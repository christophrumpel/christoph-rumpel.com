In December, I [tweeted](https://x.com/christophrumpel/status/xxx) asking what the next big leap for AI in personal use would be. We were all using AI daily — for work, for generating games for our kids, for planning trips. But what's the *next* frontier?

Two months later, I have my answer. And it came faster than I expected.

## The Question That Started It All

Most of us already use AI constantly. ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot — they've become part of our daily workflow. But I kept wondering: where does this actually *lead*? What's the endgame for personal AI use?

I wasn't expecting to find out so quickly.

Then I started using OpenClaw. And suddenly, I stopped thinking about AI as a tool I *use* and started thinking about it as an assistant I *work with*.

That shift changed everything.

## Why Apps Are Already Dead

Here's the thought that keeps me up at night: **most of the apps on your phone are about to become irrelevant.**

Think about it. Why would I pay for a fitness tracker app that:
- Has a thousand features I'll never use
- Costs €10/month forever
- Doesn't work *exactly* how I want it to
- Can't adapt to my changing needs

When instead, I could ask my AI assistant to build me one in a couple of hours. Perfectly tailored to *my* needs. Completely customizable. And if I want to change something tomorrow? I just ask.

<img class="blogimage" alt="Chat screenshot about customizing a personal app" src="/images/blog/2026/future_ai_custom.png" />

This isn't science fiction. This is what I'm doing *right now*.

## The Real Future Is Personal

I believe digital AI assistants are the future of how we interact with technology. Not just AI *tools* — AI *companions* that know you, remember your preferences, and can actually *do things* for you.

The difference is huge.

Before, I had a calendar app, a notes app, a fitness app, a flight tracker, a weather app, a task manager... the list goes on. Each one was a separate thing I had to manage, configure, and pay for.

Now? I have Jarvy.

One assistant. One conversation. Endless possibilities.

- Need to track something? Jarvy builds it.
- Want to check flights? Jarvy handles it.
- Calendar reminders? Done.
- Local internet issues? We talk about it together.

All in one place. All through natural conversation.

## From "What's Next?" to "We're Here"

<img class="blogimage" alt="Chat screenshot about the future being now" src="/images/blog/2026/future_ai_here.png" />

When I asked that question in December, I genuinely didn't know the answer. I thought maybe we'd see something interesting in the next few years.

I was wrong. We're already here.

Tools like OpenClaw aren't revolutionary because of new technology. Claude Code existed. Telegram existed. All these pieces were already there. What changed is how they're *glued together*.

That's often how the future arrives — not as something completely new, but as existing pieces finally clicking into place.

## Not Everything Will Work

I want to be clear: I'm not saying AI will replace *every* app. Some things need specialized hardware, complex integrations, or regulatory compliance that a homegrown solution can't provide.

And honestly? Not everyone wants to build their own tools. That's fine.

But for those of us who want technology that actually *fits* — that bends to our needs instead of forcing us to bend to it — we're living in an incredible time.

## The Taste of the Future

I've gotten a taste of what this could be. And I can't go back.

When I need something, I don't search the App Store anymore. I don't read reviews or compare features. I just... ask Jarvy if we can build it.

Usually, we can.

That's the future I'm excited about. Not AI that replaces us, but AI that *amplifies* us. That gives everyone the power to create exactly what they need, when they need it.

Is OpenClaw the final answer? Probably not. This space is moving incredibly fast. But personal AI assistants — ones that truly know you and work *with* you — are definitely the future.

And honestly? That future feels amazing.
